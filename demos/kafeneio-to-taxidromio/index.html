Our Story

From Post Office to Coffee House

For over sixty years, the old post office building on Larnaca Square has echoed with a different kind of correspondence — the slow, unhurried conversation of a kafeneio.

When the post office closed its doors in the early 1960s, locals reclaimed the stone building for what Cypriots do best: gathering over a bracing cup of Greek coffee, a plate of olives, and the day's news. The wooden counters where stamps were once sold became the bar where coffee is ground each morning.

To Taxidromio — "the post office" — remains a living piece of Larnaca's social fabric. Men play backgammon under the awning, regulars claim the same chair for decades, and the coffee pot (called a briki) has not stopped bubbling. This is the Cyprus that guidebooks struggle to capture.
