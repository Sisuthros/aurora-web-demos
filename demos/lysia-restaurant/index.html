Our Story

34 Years of Kleftiko

When Lysia opened in 1990, the recipe was simple: take what the village produces, cook it slowly, and serve it with warmth. That recipe hasn't changed.

Lysia sits on Dhekelia Road in Pyla, a village on the edge of Larnaca where Greek and Turkish Cypriot communities have lived side by side for generations. Our name — Lysia — comes from the Greek word for "freedom," a value we carry into how we cook.

Our lamb kleftiko is the dish that built our reputation. We marinate lamb shoulder overnight, wrap it in foil with potatoes, onions, and tomatoes, and slow-bake it until the meat falls from the bone. This is the method shepherds used in the mountains — no shortcuts, no pressure cookers, just time and fire.

With 4,849 Facebook followers and a loyal local following, we have never advertised. Word travels when the food is honest. Every guest who walks through our door is treated like family — because in a village tavern, that's the only way we know.
