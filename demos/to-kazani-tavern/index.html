Our Story

A taverna built around a living flame

For more than a century, the stone kazani — the copper still that gives this house its name — sat at the heart of an Aradippou farmstead, turning grape pomace into zivania, the spirit that warmed Cypriot winters. When the distillery closed its doors, the family refused to let the flame die. In its place they laid tables, hung the old tools on the walls, and began serving the meze the workers had always eaten: village bread, halloumi grilled over charcoal, slow-roasted lamb, and a glass of the house zivania to finish.

Today, To Kazani keeps that tradition alive. The original still stands in the dining room, the courtyard tables sit under the same vines, and every plate still starts with what the village market gives us that morning.

— The Kazani family, four generations in Aradippou
